Operational Data Integration

I started by untangling data that already existed across Salesforce, web forms, course registrations, and WooCommerce. Once records, fields, and identifiers were consistent, I could automate the handoffs, preserve customer context, and build a better follow-up process — including a new consultation pipeline in Salesforce sandbox.

01 / THE DATA FOUNDATION

First, I had to make the records make sense.

The systems were working individually. The trouble started when their records had to be matched, updated, or reported on together.

Website selections didn't always match Salesforce field formats. Product prices were stored as related price-book entries, not one neat value. A registration could involve a person, employer, course, host site, and purchase. Each tool had its own way of naming those relationships.

I traced the records through those handoffs, cleaned up field mappings and picklist values, and separated data that looked similar but meant different things. That included transforming multi-select form answers, keeping member and standard prices distinct, and using stable identifiers instead of relying on display names for matching.

The principle was simple: a successful automation run didn't count for much if it left behind the wrong record.

01 / INPUTSReportable lead values

Mapped form choices to valid CRM picklists; converted grouped checkbox values into separate selections.

02 / IDENTITYConsistent record matching

Mapped how Accounts, Contacts, courses, and registrations relate; avoided name-only matching and duplicate records.

03 / PRICINGSeparate price-book values

Handled nested Standard and member pricing records explicitly instead of treating them as one field.

04 / CONTEXTKeep the source attached

Captured intent, topic interests, and campaign parameters before they could be lost downstream.

03 / CONNECTING OPERATIONS

The same cleanup mattered beyond the first inquiry.

Once data could move reliably, the job became preserving the relationships behind each transaction.

A / PRODUCTS & ECOMMERCE

A product needs more than a name and a price.

Salesforce held the source product records and two price books; WooCommerce handled storefront and fulfillment. I used Zapier to extract the correct prices and a custom WordPress REST endpoint to validate fields, assign categories, and create or update products. Dimensions, weight, and virtual status had to be carried through so shipping worked too.

B / COURSES & REGISTRATIONS

One registration can involve several records.

I worked with the links between Accounts, Contacts, parent and scheduled courses, and Course Registrants. Keeping those relationships explicit — including host-site and location distinctions — meant the business could understand participation by person, company, course, and related purchase instead of dealing with disconnected submissions.

04 / CONSULTATION LIFECYCLE

Lead routing uncovered the next gap.

SALESFORCE SANDBOX BUILD

The consulting branch got requests to the right person, but an assigned Lead was not enough to manage work that continued for months. Staff needed to see the company, the people involved, previous engagements, and what needed attention next.

I extended the model in Salesforce sandbox: the consulting Lead converts into an Account and Contact, a custom Consultation Engagement record captures the work, and follow-up Tasks make next steps visible. Multiple Contacts can be related to an engagement without duplicating the company or losing its history.

Implementation status: Objects, relationships, flows, pages, and list views were built in sandbox. Expiration and renewal reminders were proposed as a later improvement, not a deployed feature.

05 / PUTTING THE DATA TO WORK

Cleaner records made the next decisions easier.

Staff could respond to inquiries with the source and reason for contact already attached. Product exceptions — missing pricing or fulfillment details — were easier to spot before they affected a customer. Registration relationships could support course demand and customer-history reporting. And consulting requests now had a path toward ongoing engagement tracking instead of ending at the first follow-up.

I didn't treat this as a finished BI dashboard or claim a measured revenue lift. The result was a more dependable set of operating records, with attribution and reporting fields collected early enough to be useful later.
