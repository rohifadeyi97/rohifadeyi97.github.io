01 / THE DATA FOUNDATION

The first job was cleaning up the data.

Each system had useful information. The trouble came when that information had to move between them.

Form answers sometimes reached Salesforce in the wrong format. Member and standard prices sat in related records. One course registration could involve a student, employer, location, course, and purchase. I had to sort out which fields belonged where before connecting anything else.

I fixed the form mappings, normalized picklist values, separated price-book entries, and mapped the relationships between Accounts, Contacts, courses, and registrants. I also looked for stable keys so the automations were not relying on a name that could change.

A successful run was not enough. The record had to be right when it arrived.

01 / INPUTSReportable lead values

Mapped form choices to valid CRM picklists; converted grouped checkbox values into separate selections.

02 / IDENTITYConsistent record matching

Mapped how Accounts, Contacts, courses, and registrations relate; avoided name-only matching and duplicate records.

03 / PRICINGSeparate price-book values

Handled nested Standard and member pricing records explicitly instead of treating them as one field.

04 / CONTEXTKeep the source attached

Captured intent, topic interests, and campaign parameters before they could be lost downstream.

03 / CONNECTING OPERATIONS

Registrations and products needed their own rules.

The goal was to preserve the useful details behind every sale or registration, not just move records from one system to another.

A / PRODUCTS & ECOMMERCE

Keeping the catalog in sync

Salesforce held product information and two price books. WooCommerce handled the storefront and shipping. I used Zapier to look up the correct prices, then sent the product through a custom WordPress REST endpoint. The endpoint handled matching, categories, and create-or-update logic. Weight, dimensions, and virtual status had to come through too.

B / COURSES & REGISTRATIONS

Connecting course and student records

I worked with the links between Accounts, Contacts, parent courses, scheduled courses, and Course Registrants. I also kept host site and location separate where they meant different things. Those relationships are what make it possible to report on attendance by person, company, or course.

04 / CONSULTATION LIFECYCLE

Consulting needed more than a lead.

SALESFORCE SANDBOX BUILD

The consulting lead flow solved the first response, but staff also needed to track the work after that call. An assigned lead could not show the full history of a company relationship.

In Salesforce sandbox, I built an Account-based model with a custom Consultation Engagement record, related Contacts, and follow-up Tasks. It supports more than one engagement per company and more than one point of contact without creating duplicate client records.

Implementation status: Objects, relationships, flows, pages, and list views were built in sandbox. Expiration and renewal reminders were proposed as a later improvement, not a deployed feature.

05 / PUTTING THE DATA TO WORK

The data became easier to work with.

Staff could see the reason for a new inquiry and where it came from. Products had the fields needed for pricing and shipping. Course records kept clearer links to the people and companies involved. The consulting model gave staff a place to track later work with the same client.

This was a set of practical improvements to the operating data, not a claim of increased revenue. Collecting consistent source and relationship fields also made future reporting and attribution more feasible.
