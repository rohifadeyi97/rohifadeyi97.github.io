Operational Data Integration

I redesigned the data and workflows connecting customer inquiries, training operations, and ecommerce. The work began with inconsistent records and grew into a set of integrations that improved lead follow-up, product management, and reporting readiness. It also led to a consultation-tracking model in Salesforce sandbox.

01 / THE DATA FOUNDATION

Building a reliable data foundation

The organization had tools for its website, CRM, registrations, and storefront, but the underlying records did not always agree. Automating the handoffs before fixing the data would have carried the same problems into more systems.

The issues were practical: incompatible form values, separate member and standard pricing, and registration records that needed to connect people, employers, courses, and locations. Each required a clear data model before an integration could be trusted.

I mapped source fields to Salesforce, standardized picklists and record relationships, and established stable matching keys. I also separated price-book entries so the correct pricing could be carried into ecommerce.

The goal was accurate records at each step, not simply a successful automation run.

01 / INPUTSReportable lead values

Mapped form choices to valid CRM picklists; converted grouped checkbox values into separate selections.

02 / IDENTITYConsistent record matching

Mapped how Accounts, Contacts, courses, and registrations relate; avoided name-only matching and duplicate records.

03 / PRICINGSeparate price-book values

Handled nested Standard and member pricing records explicitly instead of treating them as one field.

04 / CONTEXTKeep the source attached

Captured intent, topic interests, and campaign parameters before they could be lost downstream.

02 / WORKFLOWS IN PRACTICE

Automating the core workflows

With consistent fields in place, I automated inquiry routing and product synchronization. These workflows reduced the need for staff to re-enter information and made the next action clearer.

03 / CONNECTING OPERATIONS

Connecting customer, course, and product records

The systems needed more than a data transfer. They needed to preserve the relationships and business rules behind each registration and purchase.

A / PRODUCTS & ECOMMERCE

Synchronizing the product catalog

I linked Salesforce product records and both price books to WooCommerce using Zapier and a custom WordPress REST endpoint. The integration handles matching and updates, category mapping, shipping details, and the distinction between member and standard pricing.

B / COURSES & REGISTRATIONS

Maintaining course and participant relationships

Course records needed to support scheduling and attendance analysis across people and employers. I mapped the relationships between Accounts, Contacts, parent and scheduled courses, and registrants, keeping host-site and location fields separate where they represented different information.

04 / CONSULTATION LIFECYCLE

Managing consulting work beyond the initial inquiry

SALESFORCE SANDBOX BUILD

The consultation branch of lead routing revealed a second requirement: a way to manage ongoing client work after an inquiry became a relationship. A lead alone could not represent repeat engagements and multiple contacts at the same company.

I extended the model in Salesforce sandbox with Account-based consultation engagements, related Contacts, and follow-up Tasks. The structure supports multiple engagements and points of contact while retaining a single client history.

Implementation status: Objects, relationships, flows, pages, and list views were built in sandbox. Expiration and renewal reminders were proposed as a later improvement, not a deployed feature.

05 / PUTTING THE DATA TO WORK

More useful data for operations and reporting

Lead records preserved inquiry intent and campaign source, products carried the details needed for ecommerce, and registrations retained links to the right people and organizations. The consultation extension provided a structured way to continue work with existing clients.

These changes improved the quality and usefulness of operational records. They also created a stronger foundation for segmentation, follow-up, and future attribution reporting. I have not attributed a measured revenue increase to the project.
