01 / Overview

Investigating channel performance

Who's AUX sells across Shopify, Amazon, and TikTok Shop. Revenue had declined from a 2024 peak, but with three disconnected sales channels and no unified reporting layer, it wasn't clear which channel or which products within it were actually driving the drop. This project built the model needed to answer that question directly instead of guessing from three separate exports.

02 / Approach

A consistent view across channels

The core challenge was structural, not visual: Shopify, Amazon, and TikTok each define orders, refunds, and product identifiers differently. A star schema normalized all three into shared fact and dimension tables, so a single set of DAX measures could answer questions like "which channel" and "which product" without channel-specific logic scattered across the report.

03 / Findings

Findings from the analysis

01

Channel mix

Amazon's share of revenue grew from 4.5% in 2024 to 43.3% in 2025, even as total revenue fell. Shopify/TikTok Shop collapsed far more sharply (down 78.5% year over year) than Amazon did.

02

Product-level shift

There wasn't one, the catalog is a single SKU across the entire order history, so the decline couldn't be explained by product mix. The model made that absence itself a finding: no product diversification also meant no upsell path, reflected in a 0.8% repeat purchase rate.

03

Ad efficiency

Paid spend fell 81% between 2024 and 2025 while organic posting nearly quadrupled, and revenue still fell 64%, organic volume alone couldn't replace what paid amplification had been driving. On Amazon specifically, the fee take rate climbed from 14.8% to 33.6% over the same window the channel was absorbing a larger share of total revenue.

04

Timing

The decline began immediately after a November 2024 peak, the same month paid ad spend also peaked. But the full severity only became undeniable in 2026, when posting and ad spend both fell to near-zero simultaneously, compounding a decline that had actually started over a year earlier.

04 / REFLECTION

Lessons and next steps

TikTok's own audience-interest export listed several categories twice under slightly different taxonomy paths. A plain SUM aggregation would have shown "Music" at 118% of the audience, which is obviously wrong but easy to miss without checking totals against 100%. Next time, I'd build that kind of validation into the data-loading step itself, rather than catching it visually after a chart already looked strange.
