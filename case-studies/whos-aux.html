01 / Overview

Understanding the revenue decline

Who’s AUX sold through Shopify, Amazon, and TikTok Shop, with advertising tracked separately. When revenue fell from its 2024 peak, I needed to distinguish changes in channel mix, acquisition activity, and customer behavior rather than rely on individual platform reports.

02 / Approach

Building a reliable cross-channel view

Each platform reports orders, refunds, and product identifiers differently. I standardized those fields in a shared data model and built reusable Power BI measures, giving every channel the same reporting definitions.

03 / Findings

What the analysis revealed

01

Channel mix

Amazon grew from 4.5% to 43.3% of revenue between 2024 and 2025. That larger share reflected a major decline elsewhere: Shopify and TikTok Shop revenue fell 78.5% combined, year over year.

02

Product-level shift

The business had a single SKU throughout the period, so product mix could not explain the decline. A 0.8% repeat-purchase rate highlighted how heavily revenue depended on new purchases.

03

Ad efficiency

Paid advertising spend declined 81% from 2024 to 2025. Organic posting increased nearly fourfold, but total revenue still fell 64%. At the same time, Amazon’s fee take rate rose from 14.8% to 33.6%, putting additional pressure on margins.

04

Timing

The decline began after the November 2024 revenue and advertising peak. By 2026, both paid and organic activity had fallen to very low levels, compounding a drop that started much earlier.

04 / REFLECTION

Building stronger data checks

TikTok’s audience-interest export included overlapping categories under different labels. A straightforward sum would have shown Music interest at 118%, a clear sign that the categories were not mutually exclusive. I caught the issue while reviewing the report and would now validate those totals during ingestion, before they reach a chart.
