I built a human-in-the-loop system for a recurring Vinyl Club workflow: research connected records, turn an editorial choice into a defensible playlist, publish it across music platforms, and generate a reusable content brief — without removing the final editorial decision from the process.

01

Business problem

The recurring product needed an editorial operation that could scale with it.

A monthly vinyl concept creates more than a product-selection problem. Every cycle can require research, recommendation logic, playlist building, platform matching, publishing, manual quality control, and enough editorial context to support email, social, video, or long-form content.

Doing that manually each month would make the editorial process expensive to repeat and difficult to keep consistent. Fully automating taste would create the opposite problem: speed without enough judgment. The business use case was therefore operational — build a system that removes repetitive research and publishing work while preserving an explicit human approval point for the decisions that actually shape the brand.

02

System design

I automated the repetitive work and kept the editorial decision visible.

01 / START

Resolve the seed An album or playlist link becomes the canonical anchor for that month's research.

02 / RESEARCH

Build the shortlist Local reference data and provider signals produce connected albums with frozen evidence.

03 / PICK

Make the editorial choice The editor selects two records, then can manually adjust the generated Spotify draft before approval.

04 / PUBLISH

Ship the approved month The system publishes, records skips, and generates a deterministic content packet from the final state.

03

Data architecture

The recommendation layer is built on provenance, not a single black-box similarity score.

The system combines provider APIs with local reference data so research can continue without turning every decision into a live API call. MusicBrainz canonical metadata and relationship graphs provide durable artist, release, recording, credit, and work identities; Apple Music, Spotify, Last.fm, ListenBrainz, and existing catalog observations provide bounded discovery and playability evidence.

Candidate albums are normalized across providers, exact identifiers are frozen before the editorial decision, and track-level deduplication protects against duplicate Spotify IDs, ISRCs, alternate editions, remasters, and other false duplicates. The result is a recommendation workflow that can explain where an album came from and preserve the evidence that qualified it at the time.

04

Curation logic

The objective isn't “more similar.” It's “different enough to discover, connected enough to belong.”

01 / Bridge strength

Require a convincing reason

Credits, musical relationships, and other evidence explain why a candidate belongs in the same editorial conversation. Style similarity by itself is not enough.

02 / Listening fit

Prioritize sonic evidence

Expansion tracks are ranked primarily by listening and sonic relevance. Producer or songwriter relationships can support a choice, but cannot fill the playlist on their own.

03 / Discovery

Stop popularity from choosing

Popularity is not a positive ranking signal. Higher-recognition tracks receive a mild penalty so the engine can surface less-obvious discoveries that still fit the month.

04 / Portfolio diversity

Avoid one connector taking over

Discovery is allocated across multiple credited people and candidate artists so one prolific producer, writer, or collaborator cannot monopolize the shortlist.

05

Operational controls

The system is designed to fail safely instead of publishing something it cannot defend.

01 / Approval

Human-in-the-loop

Research and draft generation are automated, but the editor still chooses the featured records and can manually adjust the playlist before READY approval.

02 / Matching

Exact-ID first

Finalists are resolved and frozen before PICK. Duplicate Spotify IDs, ISRCs, recording fingerprints, and alternate-edition collisions are rejected before persistence.

03 / Publishing

Best-effort cross-platform

Spotify receives the complete approved draft. Apple Music receives the confidently resolved subset; unavailable tracks are skipped and reported rather than replaced with uncertain matches.

04 / State

Durable workflow history

Email events, recommendation runs, approvals, manual edits, publish state, and criteria versions are stored so a monthly decision can be reconstructed later.

06

Business value

One editorial decision now drives research, publishing, and the next layer of content.

01 / Repeatability

Turn a monthly task into a reusable system

The same workflow can be repeated from a new seed without rebuilding the research and publishing process from scratch every month.

02 / Editorial leverage

Keep judgment where it matters

The engine handles retrieval, scoring, deduplication, provider matching, and state management while the human remains responsible for the final taste decision.

03 / Channel reuse

Publish once, create around it

The final playlist produces a Monthly Content Packet with featured-record context, verified connections, discovery tracks, Substack framing, and YouTube talking points.

04 / Proof of operation

28 Spotify / 26 Apple

In one completed live run, all 28 approved Spotify tracks published successfully; Apple Music resolved 26 and safely reported the two unavailable tracks instead of substituting uncertain recordings.

07

What I learned

The useful automation wasn't replacing taste. It was making taste operational.

The hardest part of the project wasn't generating recommendations. It was deciding what evidence should be allowed to influence a recommendation, how much confidence was enough to publish, and where automation should stop. Several iterations technically worked but produced playlists that were too popularity-led, too dependent on one collaborator, or too permissive about weak relationship evidence.

The final design treats curation as a controlled decision system: deterministic research where possible, explicit uncertainty when necessary, human choice at the editorial boundary, and conservative publishing when providers disagree. That made the workflow more useful as a business system than a recommendation demo.

Why this matters beyond music: the same architecture applies to other recurring decision workflows where research can be automated but the final judgment should remain accountable — vendor selection, lead prioritization, content planning, product research, or any process that needs evidence, approval, and auditable execution.
