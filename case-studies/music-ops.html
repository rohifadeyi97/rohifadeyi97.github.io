Build story / Music workflows / Content systems

Who's AUX Music Ops: from quiet automation to a visible workspace.

The playlisting workflow already did real work, but much of it happened in scripts, databases, and email. I wanted one place to see what it found, why something was recommended, what needed approval, and where the final assets ended up.

Local dashboardPlaylist operationsContent libraryCanva + motion outputs

01 / The starting point

Automation is harder to trust when you can't see it.

The underlying autonomous playlist workflow gathers and evaluates candidates across music services. Vinyl Club has its own recommendation and draft-building process. Those pieces were useful, but reviewing their decisions and cleaning up the files meant moving across tools.

The goal of the dashboard wasn't to invent a new playlist system. It was to make the existing work legible and controllable: every step, source, draft, approval, output, and storage location in one place.

Design rule

Every output has a source

People should be able to find out where a playlist suggestion or content asset came from, what happened to it, and which files are safe to archive or clean up.

02 / What the dashboard brings together

One review flow, multiple kinds of output.

The same operational habits serve music discovery, editorial planning, and visual production.

Simplified workflow

Discovery & ideas→Review & selection→Playlist / content / design

Playlist candidates and Vinyl Club recommendations stay reviewable, while selected topics and ideas move into a unified content library that can support newsletters, YouTube concepts, carousels, thumbnails, and motion assets.

03 / Working parts

Making the workflow visible is the product.

A local internal tool, not a public streaming or publishing service.

01

Discovery and candidate review

Human approval remains part of the process, with reasons and source information available for decisions.

02

Vinyl Club recommendations

Album-driven suggestions can be reviewed and used to assemble playlist drafts.

03

Unified content library

Music ideas, newsletter and video concepts, reusable formats, and draft content live in one organized workspace.

04

Editable creative outputs

Canva pages and thumbnails, preview renders, and motion/video assets have defined destinations instead of disappearing into temporary folders.

This is a locally operated workflow. Screenshots and a public-facing walkthrough can be added without exposing credentials, private databases, or unreleased material.

04 / Why it matters

Autonomy is most useful when the decisions stay understandable.

By moving the workflow out of silent email automation and into a visible dashboard, I've made it easier to review music recommendations, build content around them, and keep the assets organized when the number of workflows grows.
