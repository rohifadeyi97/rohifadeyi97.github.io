01 / Overview

Was a subscription the right next product?

Who’s AUX was relying on individual ecommerce sales. A vinyl subscription seemed like a possible way to bring in more predictable revenue, especially as order volume started to weaken.

Before buying inventory or planning a launch, I wanted to see what the subscription would cost to acquire and retain, then compare it with other product ideas.

02 / Process

I rebuilt the first forecast

My first version included seven months of synthetic subscriber history. The charts and cohort calculations worked, but I had created the very evidence I was supposed to be testing. I scrapped that version.

The second model separates published benchmarks from assumptions about this specific business. I built Conservative, Base, and Aggressive forecasts for churn, customer acquisition cost, margin, subscriber growth, and LTV:CAC. I also scored Vinyl Club against three other possible product lines.

03 / Findings

What the scenarios showed

01 / Base case

949 subs / $33.7K MRR

In the Base case, the model reaches 949 subscribers and about $33,662 in monthly recurring revenue by month 12. LTV:CAC is 2.93x, slightly below the 3:1 target.

02 / Downside

-$9,949

The Conservative case finishes year one about $9,949 below acquisition spend, based on modeled gross profit.

03 / Upside

7.29x LTV:CAC

With lower acquisition costs and roughly 5% churn, the Aggressive case turns cash-positive around month five and models about $114.9K in first-year net contribution.

04 / Scorecard

#3 of 4

Vinyl Club had the highest modeled first-year revenue at about $251.7K, but ranked third when I also weighed margin, acquisition cost, launch time, and fulfillment effort.

04 / Recommendation

What I recommended

01

Launch accessories first

Offer small add-ons at checkout first. They can generate more revenue without requiring a separate acquisition campaign.

02

Run merch in parallel

Test limited merch releases through the existing store while checking which products the audience responds to.

03

Pilot Vinyl Club at capped scale

Start with a waitlist and small subscriber group. Measure real churn and acquisition costs before committing to a larger launch.

04

Hold creator capsules

Revisit creator collaborations after the audience and expected margins make them more practical.

05 / REFLECTION

What I would change next time

I spent too much time making the first version look like a business that already had subscribers. It looked convincing, but it was not evidence. The rebuild was a better way to evaluate a product that had not launched.

I would separate benchmarks from assumptions from the start and compare every proposal with simpler options. The purpose of the model is to help decide what to do, not to make the original idea look good.

Important model caveat: the scenario outputs are forward-looking modeled results, not observed subscription performance. The addressable audience and several business-specific conversion inputs remain assumptions until a real waitlist or pilot produces first-party data.
