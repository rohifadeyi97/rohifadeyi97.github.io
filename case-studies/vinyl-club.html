01 / Overview

Evaluating a new revenue opportunity

Who’s AUX relied on one-time ecommerce transactions. As order volume softened, a subscription model offered a potential source of recurring revenue, but it also introduced inventory, fulfillment, and retention risks.

I treated the subscription as one option among several. The model needed to compare customer economics and launch complexity against simpler opportunities, not just forecast a promising revenue line.

02 / Process

Rebuilding the model around evidence

An early version used seven months of synthetic subscriber history. It produced convincing cohort charts, but that history was not evidence of demand. I replaced it with a forward-looking model grounded in published benchmarks and clearly stated assumptions.

I developed Conservative, Base, and Aggressive scenarios for churn, acquisition cost, margin, growth, and LTV:CAC, keeping market benchmarks separate from business-specific assumptions. A weighted scorecard compared Vinyl Club with three other product opportunities.

03 / Findings

The financial outlook across scenarios

01 / Base case

949 subs / $33.7K MRR

In the Base case, the model reaches 949 subscribers and about $33,662 in monthly recurring revenue by month 12. LTV:CAC is 2.93x, slightly below the 3:1 target.

02 / Downside

-$9,949

The Conservative case finishes year one about $9,949 below acquisition spend, based on modeled gross profit.

03 / Upside

7.29x LTV:CAC

With lower acquisition costs and roughly 5% churn, the Aggressive case turns cash-positive around month five and models about $114.9K in first-year net contribution.

04 / Scorecard

#3 of 4

Vinyl Club had the highest modeled first-year revenue at about $251.7K, but ranked third when I also weighed margin, acquisition cost, launch time, and fulfillment effort.

04 / Recommendation

A staged recommendation

01

Launch accessories first

Offer small add-ons at checkout first. They can generate more revenue without requiring a separate acquisition campaign.

02

Run merch in parallel

Test limited merch releases through the existing store while checking which products the audience responds to.

03

Pilot Vinyl Club at capped scale

Start with a waitlist and small subscriber group. Measure real churn and acquisition costs before committing to a larger launch.

04

Hold creator capsules

Revisit creator collaborations after the audience and expected margins make them more practical.

05 / REFLECTION

What the rebuild clarified

The initial model was detailed, but it relied on a fictional operating history. Rebuilding it around scenario assumptions made the analysis more useful for deciding whether a real-world pilot was justified.

The lesson I would carry forward is to label assumptions early and evaluate a proposal against alternatives. The quality of a forecast is measured by how well it supports a decision, not by how attractive its projections appear.

Important model caveat: the scenario outputs are forward-looking modeled results, not observed subscription performance. The addressable audience and several business-specific conversion inputs remain assumptions until a real waitlist or pilot produces first-party data.
